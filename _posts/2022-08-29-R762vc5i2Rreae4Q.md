I am writing in the wee hours of the morning, of course having not slept my due sleep. A minor incident came to mind which I feel I would forget quickly in the days to come, taking the lack of hassle involved for granted. No, what happened deserves a bit more thought and attention than a fleeting wave of emotions through my brain, never to be repeated again. I went to a friends' house to watch the "Al-classico", a clash of Pakistan and India is the Asia Cup 2022, a fixture now more notable for the money involved than before (or maybe its just now I notice it more). 

The incident happened when I was arriving at the house, freshly dressed hoping to catch up with said friends. As I pulled in through the apartment complex, the realization dawned that parking the car here would be a non trivial task. I asked Faizan to come down and help as I could not maneuver and find a spot at the same time. Faizan comes down with his friend, one that I have not met before, and more importantly, our host for the night. He finds a spot infront of his parked car, a new Suzuki Swift in a glossy black finish, without a numberplate to boot. The task: park as tightly as I can infront of his car at a perpendicular. Harnessing my reknown for good driving skills, I hoped to make a good first impression on the new guy and then my brain switched to parallel-parking mode. I have done it a dozen times before, and always rely on this skill to let me handle my car in the tighest of parking spots. I swung and I swerved and played around in the motions. Just before the final reverse into the position, I was asked to get it even tighter. And so I did, of course I could do it. I reversed abruptly, fully focused to my left, trying to close the gap. Just then I hear a heavy screech, followed by a heavy thud, a portent of doom for any driver out there. I instinctively braked, only to realize that I had swung the front of my car heavily into a small hatchback passing by on my right. It had arrived silently and met this baleful end of my Corolla. For a few seconds, I looked at Faizan, who was now standing in front observing the scene, he looked at me, then the hatchback, then me and shook his head. I slapped my forehead, looked at my options, and accepted what was to come. Holding tightly still onto the brakes, I put the car from reverse to parking, and pulled tightly on the handbrake, and stepped out.

I saw what I had caused, and although the car behind me could have had better awareness of the situation compared to me, I knew it had to be my fault, for once. The damage to our cars being heavily asymmetrical in my favour only enforced that belief. The other driver stepped out, a woman, middle-aged, yet had a worn-out look about her. I started off "It was my fault, I am ready to own up to the damages". Before giving a response, she gets closer to my side, and glances at the points of impact. My car a badly scratched, albeit mostly on the plastic bumper. Whereas the hatchback had a large torso-sized dent in the rear left door. I prepared for the worst. Maybe a slap or two even, for being utterly oblivious to my surroundings. Definitely not the mark of a good driver. She turns around to face me, Faizan at my side, but in a more mediative position facing the both of us. 

"It's fine, you don't need to pay anything." I was dumbfounded for a few seconds. This was certainly not among the responses I was mentally prepared for. I instantly responded, "but it is clearly my fault, I _will_ pay for the damages", putting an emphasis on my bias to making reparations. She one ups me here once again, "No you don't have to pay anything, really, its fine. I could be at fault too here." Maybe she was, most likely she was not. I was beginning to feel embarrased and ashamed for my premonitions and now? I could not believe this. I added a call to action, hoping to convince her to let me pay, "Let's do this, you can note down my phone number, and whatever the repair costs are, I can pay them." "You don't have to do that, you are like my brother, my little brother. He is only the same age as you. Mistakes happen." What the hell! For one, I would not give my little brother (if I had one) a free pass on such mistakes, especially if he had the means to pay up. What was happening was a benevolence from a member of an older generation to a younger one. Something I never was prepared for until I realized I was completely and totally unprepared. Guess that makes me a cynic of sorts. However once she played the ultimate trump card, considering me along the lines of a little brother, I could not go further. I realized I had lost and she was going to have her way. She was going to let me off the hook. In fact, I never felt anywhere near the hook but stories of post-accident encounters dredged out the worst in my fears, only to be quashed in an instant. 

I do not know whether I intend to take any lessons from today. But a few key observations I may later on expand someday. 
- I had a job and money in my account, maybe my push for reparations was an outcome of the need to fulfill my needs with money lying around. One of my needs being able to pay up the cost of my mistakes. Not realizing that most of the times, paying the cost is never an allusion to money.
- Kazi seemed to like my response, I will not try to let that get to my head. I still would like to be ready to accept any mistakes I make, and gracefully.
- I don't know if I made a good or a bad first impression on Saood, our host. Only time will tell, but I really do not care at this point. 
- Accidents happen, it is our response that matters.
